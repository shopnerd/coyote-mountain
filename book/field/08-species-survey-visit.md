# Field card 08 · Species survey visit

A repeat survey of one spot or route, counted the same way each time, so seasons and years can be compared.

**People:** 1 (2 makes counting easier)  
**Tool:** coyotemountainfarm.com/topo.html · Site · species surveys  

## Before the first visit

1. **Survey method:** point count, walked route, camera trap, sound recorder or pollinator watch.
2. **Add a survey spot: tap the plan**, or **add a walked route: tap along it**. Mark the spot on the ground too.
3. Decide the time of day and length of each visit, and keep it the same every time.

## A point count

1. Arrive quietly. Wait 2 minutes.
2. Count for **10 minutes**: every bird seen or heard, and anything else you set out to count. Count individuals once.
3. Write each species and its count.

## A walked route

Walk it at a steady pace, same direction each time. Count what you see and hear within a set distance either side.

## Record it

1. Pick the spot in the list. **Date**, **minutes looking**.
2. **Start the list from records here** fills in species already recorded nearby; add the counts.
3. One line each: `species, count`.
4. **Save this visit**.

## Birds from Merlin or BirdNET

Merlin checklists saved to eBird, or BirdNET results, come in through Site · nature records · **import bird records · csv**.

## Good to know

- Counts are what you saw in the time you looked, not how many live there. The tool compares per hour of looking, within a season.
- Early morning for birds. Warm, still, sunny midday for pollinators.
- A species on the **kept private** list stays out of the survey csv. Use it for nests and rare plants.

## Done when

The visit saved with its date, minutes and counts.
