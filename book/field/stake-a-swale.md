# Field card · Stake a swale with the phone level

Put a designed swale on the ground, dead level, with a phone and a straight board.

**People:** 2 (one on the board, one staking)
**Tool:** coyotemountainfarm.com/topo.html · Make · walk the line

## Before you go

1. **Design the swale.** Study · water: turn on keyline and swales, tap the keypoint. Check the swale lines sit where you want them.
2. **Save the project** (top bar · project · save). The phone needs the same project.
3. **Charge the phone.** Walk the line keeps the screen on.

## Bring

- phone with the project open
- straight board or A-frame crossbar, about 2 m, marked at each end
- stakes or flags, one per board length, plus spares
- mallet, marker, water, gloves
- in warm months, watch for rattlesnakes in brush and rock

## Zero the phone level (once, at the start)

1. Make · walk the line · **use the phone as a level**. Allow motion access if asked.
2. Lay the phone flat on the board, long side along the board.
3. Press **zero it: read it here**.
4. Turn the phone end for end on the same spot. Press the button again.
5. It shows how far off the sensor read. That error is now cancelled.

Set **target grade** to 0 for a swale on contour.

## Find the line

1. **Which lines:** keyline and swales. **Line to walk:** the swale you are staking.
2. Press **follow my position**. Walk until the reading says under a few metres from the line.
3. Phone position is good to a few metres. Use it to get close. The board sets the level.

## Stake it

1. **First stake** at the end nearest the keypoint, or where the overflow will be. Press **drop a flag here**.
2. Put one end of the board on the first stake. Lay the phone on the board.
3. Swing the far end up or down the slope until the phone says **LEVEL** and beeps.
4. Drive a stake at the far end.
5. Move the board forward so its back end sits on the new stake. Repeat.
6. Every 5 to 10 stakes, press **drop a flag here** so the line is recorded.

## Clean up the line

Stand at one end and sight along the stakes. A stake that jumps out of line is usually a bad reading. Move it halfway back toward its neighbours and check it with the board again.

## Done when

- stakes run end to end, one board length apart
- every board length reads LEVEL
- flags are dropped along the line
- **save flags · gpx** is exported and the project is saved

## After

- Site · field notes: note anything that changes the plan (rock, a spring, a tree to keep).
- Before digging, compare the flags with the designed line on the plan. A gap of more than a few metres along the whole line means the design needs another look.
- After the first big rain, walk it: where does water pond, where does it overtop? Note it.
