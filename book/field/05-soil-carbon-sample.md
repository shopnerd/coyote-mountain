# Field card 05 · Soil carbon sample

A sample for a lab to measure soil organic carbon, repeated over the years to see whether the soil is gaining it.

**People:** 1 or 2  
**Tool:** coyotemountainfarm.com/topo.html · Site · field notes, then Study · carbon  

## Bring

- a soil probe or auger that reaches 30 cm, or a spade and a ruler
- a clean bucket
- zip bags, a marker
- the lab's sample form, if the lab has one

## Steps

1. Pick the area to sample: one orchard block, one paddock, one recovery area. Sample like with like.
2. Walk a zigzag across it. Take **10 to 15 cores**, each **0 to 30 cm** deep, into the bucket. Skip dung, ant nests and fresh digging.
3. Break up the soil and mix it well. This is one composite sample.
4. Fill a bag with about 500 g. Label it: area, date, depth 0–30 cm.
5. Air-dry it out of the sun if it will wait more than a day. Do not heat it.
6. Send it to a lab and ask for **soil organic carbon, %**.

## Record it

When the result comes back: Site · field notes, **kind** soil organic carbon, **measured by** lab test, **value** the %, placed at the middle of the area, **date** the day you sampled.

## Good to know

- Study · carbon turns a % into tonnes per hectare using a depth and a soil density. The density is a default until you measure it; a lab can do it from a core of known volume.
- Sample the same areas, same depth, same season each time. Changes of a few tenths of a percent are real only when the method stays the same.
- Measured samples are kept apart from the tool's estimates in the carbon csv.

## Done when

Bag labelled and sent, the areas marked on the plan with a note.
