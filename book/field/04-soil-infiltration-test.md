# Field card 04 · Soil infiltration test

How fast water soaks into the ground: the number that says whether a swale, a mulch or a rest from grazing is working.

**People:** 1  
**Tool:** coyotemountainfarm.com/topo.html · Site · field notes  
**Method:** single ring, after the USDA soil quality test kit  

## Bring

- a metal ring about 15 cm (6 in) across and 12 cm tall, one edge sharpened: a can with both ends cut out works
- a block of wood and a mallet
- plastic wrap
- 450 mL of water, twice (that is 25 mm, or 1 inch, over a 15 cm ring), in a measuring jug
- a timer (the phone)
- a ruler

## Steps

1. Pick a spot typical of the area. Clear loose litter; leave rooted plants.
2. Drive the ring 7 to 8 cm (3 in) into the ground with the block and mallet. Keep it level.
3. Firm the soil against the inside wall with a finger so water cannot run down the edge.
4. Line the ring with plastic wrap, pour in the first 450 mL onto the wrap, then pull the wrap out gently and start the timer.
5. Stop the timer when the surface just glistens with no standing water. This first inch wets the soil.
6. Repeat with the second 450 mL. **Time the second inch.** That is the test.

## Work out the rate

mm per hour = 25.4 × 60 ÷ minutes for the second inch

| second inch took | rate |
| --- | --- |
| 3 min | 508 mm/h |
| 15 min | 102 mm/h |
| 60 min | 25 mm/h |

## Record it

Site · field notes: **kind** soil infiltration test, **measured by** eye or tape, **value** the rate in mm/h, **note it where I stand**. Write the soil condition in *what you saw* (dry, damp, crusted).

## Good to know

- Test dry soil against dry soil. A test after rain reads slower.
- Do three tests in an area and use the middle one.
- Test the same spots each season. Mark them.

## Done when

Three rates recorded for the area, spots marked, project saved.

## After

In Study · recovery, a goal of *water soaking in* can use these notes as measurements.
