# Reading and shaping land

A studio course with Coyote Mountain: observe a real site, design the smallest intervention that does the most lasting good, stake it out, and plan how to measure whether it worked.

**For:** landscape architecture, permaculture design, environmental studies, ecology and farming students; upper-level undergraduate, graduate, or adult learners
**Length:** 15 weeks, one studio day and one field half-day a week. A 5-day intensive version is at the end.
**Tools:** coyotemountainfarm.com/topo.html and /flight.html, in a browser, free. A laptop and a phone per student or pair.
**Site:** a real piece of land each studio can walk: a school ground, a farm, a park, a community garden. Get written permission to be on it.

## What students will be able to do

1. Read a site from its terrain, water, history, soil, living things and people, and say what they observed and how.
2. Find where an intervention fits with the least conflict, with hard limits kept and tradeoffs stated.
3. Design grading, water harvesting and planting, with earthwork, water and cost worked out.
4. Tell estimates from measurements, and say which is which in their own work.
5. Put a design on the ground: stake a level line, flag a route, prepare files for a crew.
6. Plan how the design will be measured over years, against an untreated comparison.

## How each week works

Every unit follows the same loop as the tool: **observe · design · build · measure · share**.

- **Studio:** a short talk, the learn chapter or tool for the week, then work on the student's own site.
- **Field:** the field card for the week, done on the site.
- **Deliverable:** one sheet, file or short video, handed in before the next studio.

Learn chapters are in the tool's top bar (learn). Field cards are in `book/field/`.

## Final project

**One intervention, done properly:** the smallest intervention that would do the most lasting good on the site.

Hand in:

1. **Site reading:** drawing sheet with sections, the water map, baseline notes and the constraints map.
2. **The case:** options considered, including leaving it as it is, and why this one.
3. **The design:** grading, water and planting plans, the earthwork budget and the water budget, with estimates marked as estimates.
4. **The build:** the staked or flagged line on the ground, and the files a crew would need.
5. **The measure:** the monitoring plan, with an untreated comparison area.
6. **The story:** a physical model, a drawing sheet or a landscape story video of 2 minutes or less.

| --- | --- |
| site reading (weeks 1–5) | 15% |
| limits and siting (weeks 6–7) | 15% |
| design, with earthwork and water worked out (weeks 9–12) | 30% |
| on the ground (week 13) | 15% |
| monitoring plan (week 14) | 15% |
| final review | 10% |

What earns marks, in every part:

- **Evidence:** observations recorded with date, method and a photo.
- **Honesty:** estimates labelled as estimates; measurements say how they were taken.
- **Restraint:** the intervention is no bigger than the problem.
- **Buildability:** someone else could build it from what was handed in.
- **Measurability:** the plan says how anyone will know whether it worked.

## Safety and ethics

- **Permission.** Written permission from whoever looks after the land, before the first visit.
- **Field safety.** Heat, water, sun, snakes, steep ground, tools. Never alone on a remote site.
- **Local knowledge.** Ask the people who know the land, including Indigenous communities where it is their land. Credit what they share, with their permission.
- **Living things.** Sensitive species stay private (Site · species surveys · kept private). No disturbing nests or dens for a count.
- **Other people's records.** iNaturalist records belong to the people who made them. Use them to learn the site; credit iNaturalist in anything shown.
- **Drones.** Follow the local rules and permissions for flying. Only licensed or supervised pilots fly.
- **Machines.** Students stake and check. They do not operate earthmoving machines unless trained and supervised.

## Further reading

- Ian McHarg, *Design with Nature* (1969): reading a landscape in layers before designing on it.
- P. A. Yeomans, *Water for Every Farm*: keyline design.
- Bill Mollison, *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual* (1988).
- Brad Lancaster, *Rainwater Harvesting for Drylands and Beyond*, Volume 1.
- Society for Ecological Restoration, *International Principles and Standards for the Practice of Ecological Restoration*, 2nd edition (2019).
- USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service, *Soil Quality Test Kit Guide*: the infiltration test in field card 04.

## For teachers

- Start the first class with learn · play all: most students need the chapters before the tools.
- Pairs work well: one on the phone, one on the board or tape.
- The reference manual (`book/reference.md`) lists every control and what it does.
- The course, the cards and the tool are free for teaching. Adapt them, and send back what worked and what didn't.
